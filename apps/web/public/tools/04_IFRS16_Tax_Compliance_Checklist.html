Why This Checklist Matters
IFRS 16 (effective 1 January 2019) fundamentally changed how leases are recognised on financial statements. A commercially sound deal that is incorrectly classified or poorly documented can create accounting errors, tax penalties, and regulatory risk. This checklist ensures every transaction is commercially sound, properly classified, correctly measured, and fully compliant.

IFRS 16 & Tax Compliance Checklist

Work through each step in sequence. Each check must be confirmed before the transaction is considered compliant for financial reporting.

Step 1: Lease Identification
Contract identifies asset
The contract identifies a specific asset that the customer controls the use of throughout the lease period.
Right to obtain substantially all economic benefits
The lessee has the right to obtain substantially all the economic benefits from use of the asset.
Right to direct use of asset
The lessee has the right to direct how and for what purpose the asset is used throughout the period of use.
Low-value asset exemption assessed
Asset value has been assessed against the low-value threshold (generally USD 5,000) — exemption applied where applicable.
Short-term lease exemption assessed
Lease term has been assessed — short-term exemption (12 months or less) applied where applicable.
Step 2: Lease Classification
Finance vs. operating determined
Lease has been classified as finance or operating based on substance over form principles.
Ownership transfer assessed
Whether ownership of the asset transfers to the lessee at end of lease has been assessed.
Purchase option assessed
The existence of a bargain purchase option (BPO) has been assessed and documented.
Lease term vs. useful life assessed
Lease term as a proportion of the asset's useful economic life has been calculated and documented.
Present value test applied
Present value of lease payments as a proportion of fair value of the asset has been calculated.
Specialised nature assessed
Whether the asset is so specialised that only the lessee can use it has been considered.
Step 3: Initial Measurement (Lessee)
Lease liability calculated
Lease liability at commencement date has been calculated as the present value of future lease payments.
Discount rate determined
The implicit rate in the lease (or incremental borrowing rate if implicit rate not available) has been determined.
Right-of-Use asset recognised
Right-of-Use (ROU) asset has been recognised equal to the lease liability plus initial direct costs and prepayments.
Initial direct costs capitalised
Initial direct costs (legal fees, arrangement fees) have been capitalised into the ROU asset.
Lease incentives deducted
Any lease incentives received have been deducted from the ROU asset measurement.
Step 4: Subsequent Measurement
Effective interest method applied
Lease liability interest is unwound using the effective interest method at the determined discount rate.
ROU asset depreciation policy set
ROU asset depreciation policy has been set — straight-line over shorter of lease term or useful life.
Remeasurement triggers identified
Events that would require remeasurement (lease modifications, variable payment changes) have been identified.
Variable lease payments classified
Variable payments have been classified as lease vs. non-lease components and accounted for appropriately.
Step 5: Lessor Accounting
Finance lease receivable recognised
For finance leases: net investment in lease recognised as a receivable at commencement.
Operating lease asset retained
For operating leases: asset retained on lessor balance sheet and depreciated per normal policy.
Finance income recognised
Finance income is recognised over lease term using effective interest method.
Derecognition of asset (finance lease)
For finance leases: asset is derecognised from lessor balance sheet at commencement.
Step 6: Kenyan Tax Compliance
KRA deductibility assessed
Deductibility of lease payments for income tax purposes has been assessed under Kenyan tax law.
Capital allowances reviewed
Where asset is retained by lessor (operating lease), capital allowances for the lessor have been reviewed.
VAT treatment confirmed
VAT treatment of lease rentals has been confirmed — standard rate (16%) applies to most equipment leases.
Withholding tax assessed
Where applicable (international lessors), withholding tax obligations have been assessed and complied with.
Transfer pricing assessment
Where the lease is between related parties, transfer pricing rules have been considered.
IFRS 16 disclosure prepared
Required IFRS 16 disclosures have been prepared for inclusion in financial statements.
