ESG Due Diligence Checklist

Complete environmental, social, and governance due diligence for lending, investment, or advisory mandates.

Module 9
0 / 24 items completed
🌿 Environmental
  • Does the company operate in an environmentally sensitive sector or location?
  • Has the company calculated its carbon footprint — Scope 1, 2, and 3?
  • What is the company's exposure to physical climate risks — flooding, drought?
  • What is the company's exposure to transition risks — carbon regulation, fuel cost?
  • Are there current or pending environmental regulatory actions from NEMA?
  • Does the company have an environmental management system — ISO 14001?
  • Has the company set emissions reduction targets with a costed plan?
  • Does capex support or undermine environmental commitments?
👥 Social
  • What is the company's health and safety record — LTIFR, fatalities?
  • Does the company have a published human rights policy covering operations and supply chain?
  • What is the company's relationship with the communities where it operates?
  • Are workers' rights respected — fair wages, freedom of association?
  • Does the company have a formal grievance mechanism for employees and communities?
  • What is the company's gender diversity profile at all levels?
  • Are there data privacy or consumer protection risks?
  • Does the company assess supply chain social risk through supplier audits?
🏛️ Governance
  • What is the board composition — size, independence, diversity, expertise?
  • Is there a functioning audit committee with independent members?
  • Are financial statements audited by a reputable firm, free of qualifications?
  • What is the ownership structure — any concentrated ownership concerns?
  • Is there a published anti-corruption policy and whistleblowing mechanism?
  • Are executive remuneration structures aligned with long-term ESG performance?
  • Are related party transactions fully disclosed and independently approved?
  • Is ESG governance embedded at board level — dedicated committee or champion?

Greenwashing Detection Checklist

Apply the 5-step greenwashing detection framework to any ESG claim or financial product.

Module 8
0 / 15 items completed
  • Step 1 — Identify: Have you written down the exact environmental claim being assessed?
  • Step 1: Is the claim specific and falsifiable — or vague and unmeasurable?
  • Step 1: Does the claim contain potentially greenwashing terms — "eco-friendly," "sustainable," "green," "natural"?
  • Step 2 — Scope: What is included and excluded from the scope of the claim?
  • Step 2: Has the boundary been chosen to maximise positive impression — hidden trade-off?
  • Step 2: For carbon claims — which scopes are included and which are excluded?
  • Step 3 — Verify: Is the claim independently verified? By whom? Using what standard?
  • Step 3: For green bonds — is there a Second Party Opinion from a recognised provider?
  • Step 3: For carbon offsets — are credits verified under Verra VCS, Gold Standard, or Plan Vivo?
  • Step 4 — Benchmark: How does this claim compare to sector peers and recognised standards?
  • Step 4: Is there a rating from BeZero, Sylvera, or Calyx Global for carbon credits?
  • Step 4: Does the claim significantly exceed peer performance — warranting extra scrutiny?
  • Step 5 — Conclude: Does the evidence support the claim on balance?
  • Step 5: Have you documented your analysis including the claim, evidence, and conclusion?
  • Step 5: If the claim is not supportable — have you identified the required remediation?

Carbon Credit Quality Assessment

Six-point quality assessment framework for evaluating voluntary carbon credits — especially African projects.

  • Standard: Is the project registered under Verra VCS, Gold Standard, or Plan Vivo?
  • Standard: Is the methodology approved, widely used, and not under review?
  • Standard: Is the project publicly listed on the standard's registry with full documentation?
  • Additionality: Is the baseline deforestation rate or counterfactual independently and conservatively established?
  • Additionality: Is there a credible threat to the ecosystem being protected without the project?
  • Additionality: Is the methodology recent — post-2020 — and not subject to current challenges?
  • Permanence: Is the buffer pool adequately sized — typically 15–25% for nature-based projects?
  • Permanence: Are there robust mechanisms for addressing reversal events — fire, drought, political change?
  • Permanence: Is there insurance or a replacement credit mechanism for permanence risk?

  • Verification: Are there any outstanding non-conformances or corrective action requests?
  • Community: What percentage of carbon revenues flows to the host community?
  • Community: Is benefit sharing documented, transparent, and independently monitored?
  • Community: Are there any outstanding community disputes or complaints about benefit sharing?
  • Rating: Has the credit been rated by BeZero Carbon, Sylvera, or Calyx Global?

Key Formulas & Quick Reference

Essential formulas, benchmarks, and reference tables for professional ESG and sustainable finance practice.

ESG Residual Risk Score
Residual Risk = Exposure × (6 − Management) ÷ 5
Where Exposure and Management are scored 1–5. A score of 5 Exposure + 1 Management = 5.0 (Severe). A score of 5 Exposure + 5 Management = 1.0 (Low). Applied separately to E, S, and G dimensions.
IFRS 16 Lease Liability (PV of Annuity)
PV = PMT × [1 − (1 + r)^−n] ÷ r
Where PMT = monthly payment, r = monthly rate (annual ÷ 12), n = number of months. Year 1 Finance Charge = Opening Liability × Annual Rate. Depreciation = PV ÷ lease term in years.
Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)
DSCR = Net Operating Cash Flow ÷ (Interest + Principal)
Minimum covenant typically 1.25x for Kenyan solar leases. Below 1.25x = distribution restriction. Below 1.10x = event of default. EBITDA is used as proxy for NOCF in early-stage project models.
Solar Lease Monthly Payment Target
Lease Payment = Monthly Electricity Savings × 75%–85%
Ensures day-one positive cash flow for lessee. Monthly Savings = (System Size kWp × 1,400 kWh/yr ÷ 12) × KES tariff. Adjust for location — Nairobi 1,400, Mombasa 1,500, Eldoret 1,250 kWh/kWp/yr.
SROI Ratio
SROI = Net Present Value of Social Value ÷ Total Investment
Net Social Value = Gross Value × (1 − Deadweight%) × Attribution% × (1 − Displacement%) × (1 − Drop-off%). A ratio above 1:1 indicates positive social return. M-KOPA example: 2.25:1.
Carbon Intensity (Cement Example)
Carbon Intensity = Total CO2e ÷ Production Volume (tonnes)
Global average cement intensity: 0.62 tCO2e/t cement. Bahari Cement baseline: 1.15 tCO2e/t — 85% above benchmark. SBTi 2030 target for cement: reduce intensity to 0.46 tCO2e/t (25% reduction from 2022 global average).
Blended Cost of Capital
WACC = Σ (Weight_i × Cost_i) for each capital tranche
Example: 40% senior @ 12.5% + 30% DFI @ 7.5% + 15% mezz @ 18% + 15% equity @ 22% = 11.2% blended WACC. Compare to pure commercial: ~16%. Blending reduces WACC by ~5 percentage points.
Internal Carbon Price NPV Adjustment
Adjusted NPV = Base NPV − (Annual Emissions × ICP × KES/USD × Years)
Where ICP = Internal Carbon Price in USD/tonne. At ICP = USD 30, KES 130/USD, 15,000 tCO2e/yr, 10 years: Carbon cost = USD 4.5M = KES 585M. Apply to compare green vs. brown investment options.
